Abstract
Long-context LLM serving is increasingly bot-
tlenecked by KV-cache memory, especially in
resource-constrained scenarios. Among exist-
ing KV-cache compression strategies, token-
wise methods reduce cached states but risk in-
formation loss through eviction or condensa-
tion, while feature-wise methods reduce per-
token KV dimensions but can require full-
dimensional reconstruction to apply positional
embedding, limiting decoding speedups. We
introduce SlimKV, a question-agnostic joint
token-feature KV-cache compression method.
SlimKV uses low-rank-aware training to com-
press long contexts into beacon memory states
with latent KV representations, together with
layer-adaptive rank allocation. We further un-
cover a positional asymmetry: removing key-
side RoPE affects beacon and raw tokens dif-
ferently, with much smaller degradation for
beacon tokens.
Exploiting this asymmetry,
SlimKV trains beacon KV projections under
a K-RoPE-free constraint and enables latent-
space attention during decoding, mitigating re-
construction latency. On LongBench, SlimKV
outperforms baselines at 16×/32× compres-
sion and remains leading at 4×/8×, where it
retains over 96% of the uncompressed model’s
score. Needle-in-a-Haystack confirms robust-
ness across evidence positions, and efficiency
evaluation shows up to 7.34× attention speedup
and 3.38× end-to-end decoding speedup over
the uncompressed model at 128K length.

This motivates our central question:
Can joint token-feature compression overcome
single-axis limits while avoiding the reconstruction

To answer this question, we propose SlimKV, a
joint token-feature KV-cache compression frame-
work built upon beacon-based soft compres-
sion (Zhang et al., 2025). SlimKV trains beacon
KV projections directly under low-rank constraints,
allowing each beacon state to summarize historical
tokens while forming compact latent KV represen-
tations. To better utilize a fixed feature budget,
SlimKV further allocates adaptive ranks according
to layer-wise low-rank heterogeneity.
These latent representations reduce KV-cache
storage, but the reconstruction latency bottleneck
remains in RoPE-based LLMs (Su et al., 2024).
Motivated by the intuition that compressing multi-
ple tokens into a beacon state may preserve partial
positional cues in its latent representation, we in-
vestigate the role of key-side RoPE and find an em-
pirical positional asymmetry between beacon and
raw-token Keys: removing key-side RoPE affects
both types of Keys, yet beacon memory tokens ex-
hibit much smaller performance degradation than
raw tokens (Figure 2). Exploiting this asymmetry,
we train beacon KV projections under a K-RoPE-
free constraint, allowing K-RoPE to be removed
from beacon Keys at inference while preserving
RoPE for raw-token Keys and all Queries, enabling
reconstruction-free latent-space attention.
We evaluate SlimKV on LongBench, Needle-
in-a-Haystack (NIAH), and long-context decoding
latency, covering accuracy, positional robustness,
and efficiency.

Related Work
Token-wise Compression
Token-wise KV-cache
compression reduces cache size by decreasing the
number of historical states used in attention. Ex-
isting methods include prompt-level and KV-level
compression. Prompt-level methods operate on
the input context: hard prompt compression (Jiang
et al., 2023, 2024) removes selected tokens, while
soft prompt compression (Mu et al., 2023; Ge
et al., 2024; Li et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2025)
condenses long prompts into learned memory to-
kens. KV-level methods compress generated caches
through KV eviction (Zhang et al., 2023; Li et al.,
2024; Kim et al., 2025) or KV merging (Zhang
et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2024). However, deletion-
based methods may discard useful information, and
question-aware variants are less suitable when fu-
ture queries are unavailable. Soft compression and
KV merging avoid explicit deletion but still face ca-
pacity bottlenecks under aggressive compression;
KV merging also often relies on heuristic aggre-
gation. Moreover, KV-level compression reduces
decoding-time cache cost but not prefill computa-
tion for the original long prompt.

Feature-wise Compression
Feature-wise KV-
cache compression reduces per-token cache size
by shrinking feature representations or remov-
ing head redundancy.
MQA (Shazeer, 2019)
and GQA (Ainslie et al., 2023) share or group
K/V heads, while latent-KV architectures such
as MLA (Liu et al., 2024a) store K/V in a low-
dimensional latent space with decoupled positional
handling. However, MLA-style designs typically
require pre-training from scratch and are less ap-
plicable to existing GQA-based backbones. Post-
training methods such as Eigen Attention (Sax-
ena et al., 2024), Palu (Chang et al., 2025), and
MHA2MLA (Ji et al., 2025) retrofit existing mod-
els with low-rank latent KV structures. Neverthe-
less, post-hoc SVD-style decomposition may intro-
duce information bottlenecks, and low-rank Keys
often require full-space reconstruction before RoPE
and attention scoring, incurring additional latency.

Joint Token-Feature Compression
3.1
Overview
SlimKV
is
a
joint
token-feature
KV-cache
compression framework built on the Activa-
tion Beacon-style progressive token compression
pipeline (Zhang et al., 2025). The input is chunked
with beacon tokens inserted at fixed intervals; each
chunk attends to previous beacon memories, allow-
ing beacon states to absorb contextual information.
After each chunk-wise prefill, raw-token KV states
are discarded and only compressed beacon memo-
ries are cached.
SlimKV further extends this pipeline to feature-
wise compression by training beacon KV projec-
tions directly in their target low-rank form, rather
than applying post-hoc decomposition. It caches
only latent beacon Keys and Values and allocates
layer-adaptive ranks under a global compression
budget to match layer-wise heterogeneity. Figure 3
provides an overview.

4.2
Positional Asymmetry
SlimKV leverages the positional asymmetry be-
tween raw and beacon tokens. Raw tokens are lexi-
cal units aligned with token-level RoPE, whereas
beacon tokens aggregate span-level context and
cannot be faithfully tied to a single RoPE index. K-
side RoPE ablations in Figure 2 show that remov-
ing K-RoPE degrades both raw-token and beacon
Keys, but the drop is much smaller for beacon Keys.
Attention maps corroborate this pattern (Figure 5):
beacon K-RoPE removal largely preserves the di-
agonal structure, while raw-token K-RoPE removal
distorts it. Table 1 quantifies this observation, with
metric definitions provided in Appendix A.2. Fig-
ure 6 further shows that RoPE-free beacon states
reconstruct random character sequences far above
random guessing, indicating non-trivial positional
cues without explicit K-RoPE.

5.1
Experimental Setup
Models and training.
We evaluate SlimKV on
Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct (Grattafiori et al., 2024) and
Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct (Team, 2024; Yang et al.,
2024). Backbones are frozen, and training is re-
stricted to the beacon-branch QKV projections.
We follow a two-stage recipe: pre-training on
0.5B RedPajama (Weber et al., 2024) tokens, fol-
lowed by fine-tuning on LongAlpaca (Chen et al.,
2024), BookSum (Kryscinski et al., 2022), and
GPT-generated synthetic data (Zhang et al., 2025).
All samples are shorter than 20K tokens. We use
batch size 8, learning rates of 5 × 10−5/1 × 10−5,
linear decay, and no warmup. We apply the auto-
regressive loss only to non-beacon tokens, while
beacon tokens serve as compressed memory states
and are excluded from supervision.
Compression settings.
SlimKV compresses KV
cache along token and feature dimensions. To-
ken compression is controlled by the beacon in-
terval, and feature compression by beacon KV la-
tent ranks, with 2×, 4×, and 8× settings evaluated.

Question-Aware vs. Question-Agnostic.
In our
LongBench evaluation, we include SnapKV as a
question-aware baseline to contrast with SlimKV’s
question-agnostic paradigm and position SlimKV
within the broader KV-cache compression land-
scape. SnapKV leverages the known question for
targeted KV selection (Li et al., 2024), whereas
SlimKV is question-agnostic and does not rely on
the question for compression. SnapKV is well
suited to single-request, question-specific retrieval,
while SlimKV better supports multi-turn and multi-
request scenarios.
Despite SnapKV’s question-
aware advantage, SlimKV achieves higher Long-
Bench averages at 16× and 32× compression.
To highlight this distinction, we construct a
multi-request setting on QMSum (Zhong et al.,
2021), where each context is compressed once and
reused for multiple unseen questions. Full KV
achieves 25.17 ROUGE-L F1, while at 16× com-
pression, SnapKV drops to 10.39 and SlimKV-f4
reaches 24.47, retaining 97.2% of Full KV perfor-
mance. This suggests an advantage of SlimKV in
multi-request scenarios.

Removing beacon K-RoPE does degrade accu-
racy, showing that RoPE removal alone is insuffi-
cient. SlimKV instead trains beacon projections
directly in a low-rank form under the no-K-RoPE
constraint. Layer-adaptive rank allocation further
improves performance, and the full design ulti-
mately surpasses Activation Beacon.


6
Conclusion
We introduced SlimKV, a question-agnostic joint
token-feature KV-cache compression framework
for long-context LLMs.
SlimKV learns layer-
adaptive low-rank beacon KV representations and
removes key-side RoPE only from beacon Keys,
enabling reconstruction-free latent-space atten-
tion. Experiments on LongBench, Needle-in-a-
Haystack, and decoding efficiency demonstrate a
stronger accuracy-efficiency trade-off than prior
KV-cache compression methods.
Limitations
First, SlimKV is mainly evaluated on RoPE-based
language models, which represent a widely used
family of current LLM backbones.
Since our
reconstruction-free design addresses the key-side
RoPE reconstruction bottleneck in low-rank atten-
tion, its behavior under other positional encoding
schemes (Raffel et al., 2020; Press et al., 2022;
Peng et al., 2024) remains to be further examined.
Second, our experiments focus on text-only long-
context language models. Extending SlimKV to
broader architectures, such as multimodal LLMs
with visual or cross-modal KV caches, is an inter-
esting direction but requires additional validation.

A.4
Implementation Details
Training cost and hardware.
SlimKV freezes
the backbone and updates only the beacon-branch
QKV projections. All training runs are conducted
with BF16 precision on 4 NVIDIA A100 80GB
GPUs. For Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, pre-training
takes approximately 8 hours and fine-tuning takes
approximately 6 hours, with about 50GB peak GPU
memory usage. For Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct, pre-
training takes approximately 14 hours and fine-
tuning takes approximately 11 hours, with about
78GB peak GPU memory usage.
PALU settings.
PALU jointly compresses KV
cache by reducing feature dimension and numer-
ical precision, so its total compression depends
on both factors. We follow the original PALU
settings whenever the target compression ratio is
available. Otherwise, we choose the closest origi-
nal setting and decrease feature compression only
when necessary to meet the target ratio.
This
gives PALU a conservative configuration, since
we observe that PALU is more sensitive to feature-
dimensional compression than bit-width reduction.
For 4×, 8×, 16×, and 32× total compression, we
use 1.5×/6-bit, 1.5×/3-bit, 2×/3-bit, and 4×/2-bit
feature/precision settings, respectively.

A.6
Task-wise Analysis on LongBench
We further analyze SlimKV across LongBench task
categories. As shown in Table 2, SlimKV-f4 brings
the most consistent gains on document QA tasks,
including S-Doc and M-Doc, suggesting that RoPE-
free beacon memory preserves key semantic evi-
dence for retrieval-style reasoning while reducing
token-wise compression pressure under the same
total budget.
On summarization tasks, SlimKV gives smaller
but stable gains, consistent with their stronger de-
pendence on global semantic aggregation than ex-
act token positions. Few-shot and code tasks show
more backbone-dependent trends: SlimKV im-
proves clearly on Qwen2.5-14B, while gains on
Llama-3.1-8B are smaller; code tasks are also less
uniform, likely due to their stronger reliance on
local order, formatting, and structural cues.
Overall, RoPE-free beacon memory is especially
effective for semantic aggregation and retrieval-
oriented tasks, while fine-grained order-sensitive
tasks remain more sensitive to the compression
design.
